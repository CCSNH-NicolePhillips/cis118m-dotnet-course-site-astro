## 🧠 The Precision Upgrade: while → for

Week 8 gave you the **while loop** — condition-controlled iteration. This week, you add the **for loop** — counter-controlled iteration. The difference is architectural:

<div class="component-cards">
  <div class="component-card">
    <h3>🔁 while Loop</h3>
    <p>Use when you <strong>don't know how many times</strong> the loop will run. The loop continues until a condition changes — driven by external input or unknown state.</p>
    <code>while (input != "quit") &#123; ... &#125;</code>
  </div>
  <div class="component-card">
    <h3>🔢 for Loop</h3>
    <p>Use when you <strong>know exactly how many times</strong> the loop will run. The counter is declared, checked, and incremented all in one line.</p>
    <code>for (int i = 0; i &lt; 10; i++) &#123; ... &#125;</code>
  </div>
</div>

## 🏗️ Senior Architect Perspective

<div class="architect-rule">

### ⚙️ The Counted Loop Is a Precision Contract

When you write `for (int i = 0; i < N; i++)`, you are making a **contract with the CPU**: execute the body exactly N times, using `i` as the counter from 0 to N-1. This is not just a convenience — it is a design decision. It tells every engineer reading your code: "The number of iterations is known, bounded, and intentional."

🎯 **The most common failure this week will be the off-by-one error.** Starting at `i = 1` instead of `i = 0`, or using `i <= N` instead of `i < N`, causes one extra or one missing iteration. When your loop is producing wrong output, the initializer and the condition boundary are always the first two things to check.

</div>
