## 📝 Homework 06: The Decision Matrix

<p>Now that you've built decision structures with <code>if</code>, <code>else if</code>, <code>else</code>, and logical operators, it's time to demonstrate your understanding of <strong>how programs make decisions</strong>.</p>

<div class="component-cards">
  <div class="component-card">
    <h5>🔀 Decision Structures Reflection</h5>
    <p>In 3-5 sentences, explain <strong>Decision Flow in C#</strong>:</p>
    <ol>
      <li>What is the difference between using <strong>separate <code>if</code> statements</strong> vs. an <strong><code>else if</code> chain</strong>? When would you use each?</li>
      <li>Why does the <strong>order of conditions</strong> matter in an <code>else if</code> chain? What goes wrong if you check the least restrictive condition first?</li>
      <li>Explain what <strong>short-circuit evaluation</strong> means with <code>&&</code> — how does it prevent errors like <code>NullReferenceException</code>?</li>
    </ol>
    <p style="margin-top: 12px; padding: 10px; background: rgba(78, 201, 176, 0.1); border-radius: 6px; font-size: 0.9rem;">
      <strong>Tip:</strong> Think about the "Control Tower" metaphor from Section 6.4 — the first matching condition wins, and all others are skipped.
    </p>
  </div>
